Sourced facts that `docs/02-architecture.md` leans on, checked on 19 September 2026 unless the log at the end says otherwise. Three statuses appear in that log. *Verified by fetch today* means I opened the URL on 19 September and the figure is quoted from the page. *From notes of 18 September, to re-check* means the page wouldn't open today (or I didn't reopen it), so the prose rounds it. *Rounded* means it's my arithmetic on a verified figure. ## 1. Prior art

i.AI's consultation tool is public. The `i-dot-ai/consult` repository is public and MIT-licensed (GitHub API, 19 September 2026). Its `AGENTS.md` names the stack: Python 3.12, Django 5.2, PostgreSQL with pgvector, Redis and Django RQ, an Astro and Svelte frontend, AWS ECS, Batch, Lambda and S3, Terraform. The consultation lifecycle runs SETUP, FINDING_THEMES, FINALISING_THEMES, ASSIGNING_THEMES, ANALYSIS. The domain model separates `CandidateTheme` (AI) from `SelectedTheme` (human-approved) and records `assigned_by` on each response-to-theme annotation.

I read it before designing because it's public and it's the product the brief describes. Where I've kept a decision it's because I agree with the reasoning; where "every department" changes the problem I've gone further. The decision records are cited here as public documents and nothing more.

- **ADR 0002, Database design for consultation data and AI pipeline outputs** (27 January 2025, accepted). Two versioning strategies: `django-simple-history` on response-to-theme mappings, and new rows for Theme and Framework on every change so the theme state at assignment time survives. https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0002-database-design-for-consultation-data-and-ai-pipeline-outputs.md
- **ADR 0004, Frontend/backend choices for dashboard** (15 May 2025, accepted). The dashboard assumes JavaScript with a minimal fallback page; filters and pagination travel as a query string and the backend returns JSON, because "with a lot of results (potentially over 100k) page loads take a long time when not paginated". https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0004-frontend-backend-choices-for-dashboard.md
- **ADR 0006, Database redesign for self-serve data setup** (18 February 2026, accepted). One `response` table holds every answer as text whatever the question type; a multi-select answer becomes one row per chosen option; `closed_question_id` links an open question to its paired closed one. Benchmark on 500,000 respondents by 20 questions (10 million rows): a closed-question frequency count in 68 ms, open responses filtered by three closed values in 218 ms. https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0006-database-redesign-for-self-serve-data-setup.md
- **ADR 0007, Consolidate job processing onto SQS** (30 March 2026, accepted). Context: a pipeline grown across Redis Queue, AWS Batch, EventBridge, Lambda and S3, with separate failure domains, no local end-to-end path, and consultation-scoped jobs where one failure forces a full rerun. Decision: one SQS queue for AI pipeline jobs, a dedicated worker service, PostgreSQL as the authoritative store, and a `jobs` table. https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0007-consolidate-job-processing-onto-sqs.md
- **ADR 0008, Per-question pipeline with DB as source of truth** (30 March 2026, accepted). One job per question. `Question.status` runs draft, configured, finding_themes, assigning_themes, finalising_themes, confirmed_themes, error. The `jobs` table records execution metadata and outcomes; S3 leaves the critical path and becomes optional archive. https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0008-per-question-pipeline-with-db-source-of-truth.md
- **ADR 0009, Merge themefinder into consult as a uv workspace** (1 May 2026, accepted). themefinder becomes a workspace member of the main repository to remove release coordination between two repositories; PyPI publishing continues from there. https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0009-merge-themefinder-into-consult-as-a-uv-workspace.md
- **ADR 0010, Observability improvements** (5 August 2026, accepted). OTel-format logs, trace ids that link logs across the stack, alerts to Slack, Langfuse for LLM tracing. https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0010-observability-improvements.md
- **ADR 0011, Evaluation tooling** (5 August 2026, accepted). Langfuse for evaluation and datasets, pydantic-evals for LLM-as-judge scoring, LiteLLM as the model provider. It says "the model we use is nearing deprecation" and aims "to switch before October 2026", with a revisit "in roughly Sep 2026". https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0011-evaluation-tooling.md
- **ADR 0012, Minimum self-serve** (6 August 2026, accepted). Three permission tiers (superuser, consultation manager, consultation user), direct upload of Qualtrics or Citizen Space exports, a data checklist, and confirming question types before a consultation is confirmed. Risks named: too many simultaneous users overwhelming the pipeline, and scope creep. https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0012-minimum-self-serve-decision.md
- **ADR 0014, two files carry the number.** *DVC for eval pipeline version control* (21 August 2026, accepted) makes evaluation runs reproducible DAGs that only re-run what changed. *Sentry vs logging for exceptions* (9 September 2026, in review) has Sentry capture only uncaught exceptions and leaves handled errors to logs. https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0014-dvc-for-eval-pipeline-version-control.md and https://github.com/i-dot-ai/consult/blob/main/docs/architecture/decisions/0014-sentry-vs-logging-for-exceptions.md

**themefinder** (`i-dot-ai/themefinder`, MIT, documentation Crown copyright under OGL 3.0) is the analysis library. Its README lists the stages in order: sentiment, theme generation, theme condensation, theme refinement, optional target alignment, theme mapping. My notes record the defaults in its `tasks.py` (generation batches of 50 responses, condensation 75, mapping 20, concurrency 10, six retries with 1 to 20 second backoff, failed rows retried one at a time); those are to re-check before any number from them is quoted. https://github.com/i-dot-ai/themefinder

What I kept, on my own reasoning: a queue, a worker and Postgres as the source of truth; per-question jobs with a jobs table and concurrency caps; candidate versus approved themes with a human gate; a JSON filter API with server-side pagination; a provider-agnostic gateway with the model recorded per job; OTel logs with LLM tracing. Where I've gone further: tenancy and fairness per department; immutable theme-set versions; validate and estimate before any spend; an agreement number from human edits; a public-input threat model; GOV.UK Notify; closed-question analysis and a report export. Three mechanisms the ADRs don't spell out, which I make explicit: the fan-in, effectively-once email, and the sign-off guard server-side.

## 2. Public evaluation and cost figures

Two public sources say different things about the same programme and I keep them apart.

**The press release** (DSIT and Defra, 16 October 2025) reports that Consult categorised "over 50,000 responses" to the Independent Water Commission's call for evidence "in around 2 hours, costing £240", and that "experts only needed 22 hours to check the results". It carries the government-wide claim of "75,000 days of manual analysis" a year and "£20 million in staffing costs". It also repeats the evaluation's headline: an F1 of 0.79 to 0.82 against human inter-rater agreement of 0.74. https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ground-breaking-use-of-ai-saves-taxpayers-money-and-delivers-greater-government-efficiency

**The DWP Pathways to Work evaluation report** (i.AI, dated 27 August 2025, PDF on gov.uk) is the technical source. 87,738 responses to consultation questions were reviewed by 125 people. Consult against a single reviewer scored F1 0.816; two reviewers against each other scored 0.710. Consult's mappings were left unchanged for 73% of responses, where two reviewers gave identical mappings for 65%. The median review time was 19 seconds, with 78% of responses reviewed in under a minute. It also states that "each year the government runs around 600 public consultations, with some receiving more than 100,000 responses". https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68f63fe01c9076042263f0bd/evaluation-report.pdf

**The Independent Water Commission evaluation** on ai.gov.uk returned HTTP 403 today, both the page and its PDF. My 18 September notes record: F1 0.79 and 0.82 against two reviewer groups versus 0.74 between the groups; for roughly four responses in five at least one reviewer made no change; reviewers added a new theme to roughly one response in eight; median review time about a quarter of a minute. To re-check. Only the F1 figures are corroborated, by the press release above. https://ai.gov.uk/evaluations/consult-evaluation-independent-water-commission-call-for-evidence/

**The ATRS record** for Consult (DSIT, 14 May 2025) names GPT-4o on the Azure OpenAI service, states that "none of the consultation data can be seen by Microsoft or OpenAI or be used to train future models", puts all cloud processing inside Cabinet Office AWS and Azure environments with workloads in the London AWS region, lists the phase as pre-deployment, says each department using the tool must complete its own DPIA, and gives "400 times cheaper and over 120 times quicker (3 minutes vs 6 hours on this sample)". https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/dsit-consult

What I take from these. £240 for 50,000 responses puts model spend two orders of magnitude below the 22 hours of expert checking, so human review time is the cost that matters, and the sign-off screen is where it's spent. Reviewers adding themes the model missed is the coverage signal that matters, not a low "Other" rate. In the submission I cite one of these numbers, rounded.

## 3. UK government platform constraints

**GOV.UK Notify.** A live service may send 250,000 emails a day. The API allows "3,000 messages per minute". A file sent by email "must be smaller than 2MB" and its download link lasts 26 weeks by default. Delivery callbacks report `delivered`, `permanent-failure`, `temporary-failure` or `technical-failure`. Data is "stored and processed in AWS data centres in the UK and Ireland", with sub-processors in the UK and EEA; Notify is designed for OFFICIAL including OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE and "must not be used to process data classified as SECRET"; message content is kept for seven days by default. So the completion email carries a consultation name and a link, never response text. https://www.notifications.service.gov.uk/using-notify/daily-limits, https://docs.notifications.service.gov.uk/python.html, https://www.notifications.service.gov.uk/features/security

**GOV.UK Frontend and WCAG 2.2 AA.** The accessibility regulations guidance (updated 30 September 2024) requires public sector bodies to meet "WCAG 2.2 AA", states that "intranet and extranet websites are covered by the accessibility regulations", and requires a published accessibility statement. The GOV.UK Design System's own statement (reviewed 18 December 2024) says the GOV.UK Frontend codebase "is fully compliant with" WCAG 2.2 AA. An internal dashboard for civil servants is in scope, which is why the wireframes use GOV.UK Frontend components and tables first, charts as enhancement. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/accessibility-requirements-for-public-sector-websites-and-apps, https://design-system.service.gov.uk/accessibility-statement/

**Classification.** The Government Security Classifications Policy (30 June 2023, updated 5 August 2024) defines OFFICIAL as the majority of public-sector information "which could cause no more than moderate damage if compromised". The -SENSITIVE marking is a handling marking on OFFICIAL, not a separate tier, for information "not intended for public release and that is of at least some interest to threat actors". Access "must be no wider than necessary for the efficient conduct of official work". Consultation responses are OFFICIAL, some of them SENSITIVE, and the design treats all of them the same way. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-security-classifications/government-security-classifications-policy-html

**Where OFFICIAL may be processed.** GDS's multi-region cloud guidance (5 February 2025): OFFICIAL data including SENSITIVE "can be stored and processed in data centres or Cloud regions overseas when satisfactory legal, data protection and security practices are in place; there is no universal requirement for government data classified as OFFICIAL to be physically located in the UK", and "you do not need to carry out a Transfer Risk Assessment if you are making a transfer to any country covered by UK adequacy regulations". That's why the design records the inference region per job rather than fixing it to London, and why data at rest stays in London regardless. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/multi-region-cloud-and-software-as-a-service/multi-region-cloud-and-software-as-a-service-html

**NCSC on prompt injection** (blog, 8 December 2025): "there's a good chance prompt injection will never be properly mitigated" the way SQL injection was; protections should "focus more on deterministic (non-LLM) safeguards that constrain the actions of the system"; "when an LLM processes information from a party, the privileges it has drops to that of the party"; and you should be logging "full input and output of the LLM". The model in this design has no tools, its output is an enum of theme keys checked in code, every sent response id must come back exactly once, and responses reach the model as JSON-encoded data after a preamble that says instructions inside them are data. https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/prompt-injection-is-not-sql-injection

**AI Playbook for the UK Government** (10 February 2025). Principle 4, meaningful human control: "humans validate any high-risk decisions influenced by AI", and "you should fully test the product before deployment" with "regular checks of the live tool in place". Principle 3 names "prompt injections and hallucinations" as AI-specific threats. The per-question sign-off and the calibration sample are the design's answer to principle 4. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-playbook-for-the-uk-government/artificial-intelligence-playbook-for-the-uk-government-html

**ATRS** (mandatory scope and exemptions policy, 17 December 2024): ministerial and non-ministerial departments and arm's-length bodies must publish a record for tools that "have a significant influence on a decision-making process with public effect" or "directly interact with the general public", once at beta, pilot or production. Consult's record (section 2) describes a pre-deployment phase; a production service would publish its own current record, and the manifest export pre-fills the fields. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-mandatory-scope-and-exemptions-policy/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-atrs-mandatory-scope-and-exemptions-policy

Two more from the notes, to re-check: the Cabinet Office Consultation Principles (2018) ask for responses to be published within 12 weeks of closing, which sets the export deadline; and UK consultation guidance says the public in Wales may respond in Welsh and processes must exist to handle it, which is why the Welsh lane is a dated gap and not an exclusion. https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5ae6d71ae5274a702130dc30/Consultation_principles_.pdf, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/822901/2019-08-01_WEB_PUB_.pdf

## 4. Technical facts the design leans on

The Postgres pages are the current docs (PostgreSQL 18). Each behaviour predates the 17 the proof-of-concept runs on.

**READ COMMITTED and FOR UPDATE.** When an UPDATE or SELECT FOR UPDATE finds its target row already updated by a concurrent transaction, "the would-be updater will wait for the first updating transaction to commit or roll back", and if that one committed, "the search condition of the command (the WHERE clause) is re-evaluated to see if the updated version of the row still matches the search condition"; for SELECT FOR UPDATE "it is the updated version of the row that is locked and returned to the client". This is the fan-in. Two workers finishing the last two questions both lock the consultation row; the second waits, sees the first's commit, and counts correctly. https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html

**SKIP LOCKED.** "Any selected rows that cannot be immediately locked are skipped", which gives "an inconsistent view of the data" but "can be used to avoid lock contention with multiple consumers accessing a queue-like table". The notification outbox relay uses it; nothing else in the schema does. https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-select.html

**NULLS NOT DISTINCT.** "For the purpose of a unique constraint, null values are not considered equal, unless NULLS NOT DISTINCT is specified." Any unique key in the schema with an optional column needs the clause, or duplicates slip through on the null. https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-createtable.html

**jsonb_path_ops.** The non-default GIN operator class supports only `@>`, `@?` and `@@`, but "a jsonb_path_ops index is usually much smaller than a jsonb_ops index over the same data, and the specificity of searches is better"; it produces no entries for structures with no values, such as `{"a": {}}`. The filter query uses containment only, so it takes the smaller index. https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/datatype-json.html

**SQS visibility timeout.** Default 30 seconds. "The visibility timeout has a maximum limit of 12 hours from when the message is first received. Extending the timeout doesn't reset this 12-hour limit." Under at-least-once delivery "Amazon SQS doesn't guarantee that a message won't be delivered more than once within the visibility timeout period". So the queue is a doorbell: the lease, the heartbeat and the fence live in the jobs table, and a 100,000-response question that runs past 12 hours is unremarkable. https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSSimpleQueueService/latest/SQSDeveloperGuide/sqs-visibility-timeout.html

**ECS stopTimeout.** "Time duration (in seconds) to wait before the container is forcefully killed if it doesn't exit normally on its own"; default 30 seconds, maximum 120, on Fargate platform 1.3.0 or later. A worker gets two minutes from SIGTERM to finish its batch and checkpoint, which caps the batch size on the synchronous lane. https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonECS/latest/developerguide/task_definition_parameters.html

**Azure OpenAI gpt-4.1 prices** (Azure Retail Prices API, region uksouth, meters effective 1 April 2025, per million tokens): Global Standard input $2.00, cached input $0.50, output $8.00; Global Batch input $1.00, output $4.00. There's no cached-input meter under Batch for gpt-4.1, so the two discounts don't stack for that model. My notes add Data Zone at about a tenth more and Regional at about a fifth more; to re-check. The cost table in `docs/05` uses the Global Standard and Batch meters at £0.75 to the dollar. https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices?$filter=armRegionName%20eq%20%27uksouth%27%20and%20contains(productName,%20%27OpenAI%27)%20and%20contains(meterName,%20%274.1%27)

**Azure Global Batch** (doc dated 13 May 2026): "a 24-hour target turnaround at 50% less cost than global standard"; `completion_window` must be `24h`; jobs that run longer "continue to execute until canceled" and you pay for completed work; 100,000 requests and 200 MB per input file; uksouth is in the Global Batch region table. The region-availability page (dated 3 September 2026) says Standard/Regional deployments are "processed in the region associated with your deployment (not available for batch deployments)", so a UK-only consultation has no batch lane and runs synchronously. Which models have Regional Standard in UK South changes often; the notes' list is to re-check. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/how-to/batch, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/foundry-models/concepts/models-sold-directly-by-azure-region-availability

**Azure prompt caching** (doc dated 11 August 2026): a request needs "a minimum of 1,024 tokens" and "the first 1,024 tokens in the prompt must be identical"; hits appear as `cached_tokens` under `prompt_tokens_details`; every GPT-4o-or-newer model qualifies; models before the GPT-5.6 family don't charge for cache writes; gpt-4.1 can keep a cache for up to 24 hours with extended retention. So the mapping prompt puts the shared theme list first, and a theme list shorter than that won't cache at all. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/openai/how-to/prompt-caching

**Model retirement** (Microsoft Foundry schedule dated 2 September 2026, updated 14 September): gpt-4o 2024-05-13 retires on 1 October 2026 (replacement gpt-5.1); gpt-4o 2024-11-20 and gpt-4.1 2025-04-14 are Legacy and retire on 14 April 2027; text-embedding-3-large retires on 9 February 2028. A model alias pinned per consultation, with the manifest recording both aliases if a consultation is reopened on a new one, is the design's response. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/model-retirement-schedule

**LiteLLM.** The unified `/v1/batches` endpoint supports OpenAI, Azure, Vertex and Bedrock; Anthropic isn't listed; automated batch cost tracking is marked Enterprise-only, so the app records usage per job itself. Prompt caching passes `cache_control` through for Anthropic and "automatically translates" it to Bedrock's `cachePoint`; OpenAI and Azure caching is automatic. The notes record v1.101.0 (15 September 2026) as the latest stable release, to re-check; the deployed gateway version is the first question for the platform team. https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/batches, https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/completion/prompt_caching

## 5. Real consultation scale

- **How many.** The DWP evaluation says around 600 public consultations a year. The gov.uk search API counts 397 consultations first published in 2025. Either is fine as a planning figure: one or two new consultations per working day across government. https://www.gov.uk/api/search.json?filter_content_purpose_subgroup=consultations&filter_first_published_at=from:2025-01-01,to:2025-12-31&count=0
- **Independent Water Commission call for evidence** (final summary of responses, 2025): 50,114 responses across 73 questions; 1,605 via Citizen Space, 162 by email, 2 by post, 15,741 via the Organise campaign, 970 via WildFish, 28,458 via the joint 38 Degrees and Surfers Against Sewage campaign. Nine responses in ten came through campaigns (rounded). The report says a government AI-assisted tool, Consult, supported theme identification. https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/687d4ecaa5561a5a7e726bd1/Independent_Water_Commission_-_Summary_of_Responses_to_the_Call_for_Evidence.pdf
- **Creating a smokefree generation and tackling youth vaping** (government response, 12 February 2024): "118,756 responses", of which "90,835 were unambiguously identified as having been submitted by automated programmes", detected because the data was incompatible with the survey flow and arrived at high frequency from a small number of IP addresses; 27,921 were analysed. https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/creating-a-smokefree-generation-and-tackling-youth-vaping/outcome/creating-a-smokefree-generation-and-tackling-youth-vaping-consultation-government-response
- **Growing up in the online world** (response totals, 4 June 2026): 116,211 responses across all routes, 33,141 of them campaign submissions, 24,534 full questionnaires; hosted on SmartSurvey and Savanta. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/growing-up-in-the-online-world-consultation-response-totals/growing-up-in-the-online-world-consultation-response-totals
- **Reform of the Gender Recognition Act** (analysis of responses, 2020): 103,833 submissions, 102,818 valid after de-duplication; 27 closed and 28 open questions; around 860,000 free-text answers totalling over 37 million words (about 43 words each, rounded), some single answers of 4,000 words and some pasted journal articles; 64% arrived via third-party forms rather than official channels. https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f69ba7ee90e077f5b8be9cd/Analysis_of_responses_Gender_Recognition_Act.pdf
- **Consult ADR 0006 benchmark** (section 1): 500,000 respondents by 20 questions, 10 million rows, 68 ms and 218 ms for the two dashboard queries.

The shape that matters: the median consultation is small, the tail is enormous, and in the tail campaigns and bots can be most of the raw rows. That's why the design validates and costs before spend, flags campaign clusters rather than dropping them, and benchmarks the filter query at the 100,000-respondent mark rather than the median.

cell cap is still Excel's 32,767 characters. The row is left as checked;
this note is the correction.
